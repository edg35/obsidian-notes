# The 6 Principles of Diet and Health

> [!NOTE]
> Diet and health interaction can be separated into 6 distinct dietary factors

1. [[Calorie Balance]]: How much food you are eating per average day in  relationship to how much energy you are burning on  body processes and physical activity.
	1. See [[Calories]]
2. [[Food Composition]]: The sources of the food you are consuming; be it  animal or plant proteins, fibrous carbs or simple  sugars, the kinds of fats you are taking in, and how  processed vs. unaltered the food sources are. 
3. [[Macronutrient Amount]]: How much of each of proteins, carbs, and fats you are  eating on average per day.
4. [[Nutrient Timing]]: How often you eat and how you space out your meals  and snacks.
5. [[Hydration]]: How much fluid you take in from food and drink.
6. [[Supplements]]: What kinds of extra pills, powders or extracts you are  taking to enhance your diet.

# Unequal Effects

All of the principles of diet affect the health value of every diet. Each principle has different magnitudes of effects on how much changes to them affect the health effects of the diet.

If all principles had equal weight, Then we could come to some pretty crazy conclusions:
- You can eat all the food you want, so long as it’s timed correctly. 
- If you just take certain supplements, you can eat only junk food and be quite  healthy. 
- If you eat only healthy foods, you can be 200lbs overweight and not suffer any ill effects.

6 principles of dieting affect  health to the following extents: 

| [[Calorie Balance]]      | 60%  |
| ------------------------ | ---- |
| [[Food Composition]]     | 20%  |
| [[Macronutrient Amount]] | 10%  |
| [[Nutrient Timing]]      | 5%   |
| [[Hydration]]            | 2.5% |
| [[Supplements]]          | 2.5% |

# Principles as Priorities

Two main errors from assuming all principles are equally important:
1. Error 1: You Spend a majority of your time and effort focusing on princples that will have little to know impact on your overall healthy.
2. Error 2: Because of error 1, you glance over the big picture and fail to make changes to what matters most.

Side Effect Risk: The big side effect from committing the two errors above is not  to be understated. The side effect is that after spending so much of your time  and energy on altering your diet and getting so little out of it, you might become  frustrated not just with your most recent approach to dieting, but with the idea  of dieting as a whole.

- It is best to views these as diet priorities, and you should master the most important before moving down the table

# Path to a Healthier Diet

> [!NOTE]
> Four main recommendations will go a long way to success

#### [[Adherence]]
- The ability to stick to a diet is vital since you will not be able to reap the benefits if you can not stick to it. The best healthy diet is one you can stick to.
#### [[Progression]]
- Start small, since going all in from the beginning is overwhelming and likely to lead to failure. Overtime with [[Adherence]], increase the difficulty of the diet.
#### [[Resilience]]
- When one fails (life happens), It is important to not let your skid become a two day, week, or month thing, but that you get back on and keep eating healthy, Health benefits from diet take a while to take effect.
#### [[Sensibility]]
- Long-term healthy eating is best achieved by aiming to eat well most of the time, rather than striving for perfection, to avoid psychological strain and burnout. Allowing occasional indulgences helps prevent "diet fatigue," refreshing motivation and sustaining healthy habits over the long term. Remember to practice [[Resilience]].
