> [!NOTE] Definition
> How much food you are eating per average day in  relationship to how much energy you are burning on  body processes and physical activity.

#### What are Calories?

[[Calories]]: In the very simplest sense, calories are just a unit with which to measure energy,  specifically the energy people get from eating food.

#### Calories and Bodyweight

> [!NOTE]
> Calories Balance is the ONLY determining factor for long term bodyweight

- No matter how well you follow the other principles of healthy dieting such as [[Nutrient Timing]], good [[Hydration]], [[Food Composition]], taking the best [[Supplements]], and ideal [[Macronutrient Amount]]s, the only determinate of bodyweight will be calories in vs out.
- If you want to maintain weight, you need to make sure you are, on average, eating the same amount of calories as you burn

 > [!NOTE] More Info
> - Body Fat levels seems to be the best predictor of overall health when compared with BMI and bodyweight
> - See [[The Diet Priorities for Health]] for pyramid and total effect on health

#### Bodyweight and Health

- **Caloric Intake and Health**:
    
    - Adequate caloric intake is essential for energy and supporting body structures critical to survival and health.

- **Bodyweight as an Indicator**:
    
    - Bodyweight provides a rough measure of energy and structural support intake.
    - Being within a healthy weight range for one's height suggests adequate caloric intake.

- **Health Implications of Bodyweight**:
    
    - Significant deviations from the healthy weight range (either much less or more) indicate potential health compromises.

- **Calorie Balance and Health**:
    
    - Calorie balance significantly impacts health, mainly through its effect on bodyweight.
    - Calorie balance accounts for roughly 60% of the total health effect of diet due to the importance of bodyweight and body fat levels.

##### Realities

> [!NOTE]
> Bodyweight and [[Calories]] have a near 1:1 relationship. If Bodyweight is too low or high, health will suffer.
> 

**Low Bodyweight and Health**
-  Chronic Under-Eating:
    
    - Leads to significant weight loss and deteriorating health before reaching starvation.
    - Results in weakened immune system, making individuals more prone to infections and slower recovery.
- Impact on Muscles and Bones:
    
    - Underweight individuals suffer from muscle and bone weakness.
    - Increased risk of fractures and conditions like [[osteopenia]] and [[osteoporosis]], particularly in the elderly.
- Physical Activity and Health:
    
    - Reduced strength and increased injury risk hinder physical activity.
    - Lack of activity can negatively impact health and longevity, especially in older adults.

**High Body Weight and Health**

-  Prevalence of Overweight vs. Underweight:
    
    - In modern western countries like the U.S., more people suffer health issues from being overweight than underweight.
    - About 2% of Americans are underweight, while over 70% are overweight.
- Health Impact of Excess Body Fat:
    
    - Excess body fat, primarily due to overeating and lack of activity, is a major health issue.
    - Obesity-related diseases significantly impact the health of the average westerner more than other factors, including smoking, infectious diseases, and workplace accidents.
    - Some impacts on health include but are not limited too:
	    - High body weights literally weigh down on body structures (like the knees and  hips) leading to their faster deterioration. 
	    - Large tissue masses require more blood to be pumped through them, which  makes the heart work harder without a break, leading to increased risk of  heart trouble.
	    - Heavier people tend to have higher blood pressures. Chronically high blood  pressures literally damage the kidneys and other organs, directly leading to health maladies.
	    - Heavier people tend to have poorer glucose dynamics, which means they  develop [[pre-diabetes]] and [[type 2 diabetes]] much sooner than lighter people. 
	    - Body fat stores themselves secrete a variety of hormones and lipids, in such raising triglycerides and  LDL (bad cholesterol) levels and lowering HDL (good cholesterol) levels. 
	    - High levels of body fat cause a higher level of chronic #inflammation in the body.

#### Implications

##### Calories as a Priority

- **Calorie Balance and Weight Management**: Simply maintaining a healthy weight through calorie balance can provide significant health benefits, even if the diet includes unhealthy foods or lacks nutritional balance.
    
- **Health Improvement through Calorie Reduction**: Reducing calorie intake or increasing physical activity to lose weight can lead to substantial health improvements without necessarily changing the types of foods consumed.
    
- **Case Study of Extreme Diet**: Dr. Mark Haub's experiment with a diet of snack cakes (Twinkies, Ho-Hos) showed significant health improvements due to caloric deficit, despite poor nutritional quality. This underscores the paramount importance of calorie balance over other dietary factors. #CaseStudy
    
- **Minimal Nutritional Requirements**: While calorie balance is critical, meeting minimum levels of protein and micronutrients is still necessary for health.

- **Calorie Management vs. Other Nutritional Factors**: Calorie intake has a significant impact on health, accounting for 60% of the total health outlook. Simply managing calorie intake can lead to better health outcomes than focusing solely on healthy food choices, macronutrient ratios, timing, supplements, and hydration without addressing calories.
    
- **Healthier Eating vs. Weight Loss**: While eating healthier foods is beneficial, it does not compare to the health benefits of losing weight through calorie reduction. Overweight individuals who eat healthier but do not lose weight will not see as significant health improvements as those who focus on calorie reduction.
    
- **Priority of Calorie Balance**: The most effective approach for health is both eating healthier and losing weight. However, if one must choose, focusing on calorie reduction alone can be more beneficial for health than eating healthier foods without losing weight.

##### Weight Loss Goals and Health

- Usually no matter how small, weight loss for the overweight will bring some form of health benefit

- Many will try to lose a lot of weight in one shot in an all or nothing effort, however it is beneficial not to give up ([[Adherence]], [[Resilience]]). Continuous weight loss is much more important for health.

- Longevity in weight loss is much more important then quickly losing weight to gain it back. [[Adherence]] to any diet plan you choose is vital to maintain the healthy benefits.

##### How many Calories do you NEED for health?

- **Healthy Weight Range**: Bodyweight should be within a certain range based on height. The specific range (e.g., 150-180 lbs) will be discussed in more detail later.
    
- **Caloric Needs and Weight Maintenance**: The number of daily calories required to maintain a healthy weight depends on various factors, not just body weight.
    
- **Factors Affecting Calorie Needs**:
    
    - **Daily Physical Activity**: More active jobs require higher calorie intake compared to sedentary jobs.
    - **Exercise**: Regular exercise increases calorie needs without leading to weight gain.
    - **Genetics**: Metabolic rate differences mean some people naturally burn more or fewer calories.
    - **Height**: Taller individuals need more calories due to greater surface area and heat production.
    - **Muscle Mass**: More muscle mass leads to slightly higher calorie requirements, even at the same weight.
    - **Gender**: Males typically burn more calories than females due to hormonal and muscle mass differences.
    - **Age**: Calorie needs generally decrease with age, primarily due to muscle mass loss. Exercise can slow this decline.

- **Individual Variations**: Two people of the same weight may have different caloric needs due to the above factors. Thus, maintaining a healthy weight involves individualized calorie intake, which can vary significantly between individuals.

- **Individual Calorie Needs**: You need to figure out your own calorie requirements rather than relying on someone else's.
    
- **<span style="color:rgb(255, 182, 193)">Practical Method for Calorie Estimation</span>**:

> [!NOTE]
> Practical starting point for someone trying to lose weight
> 
> 


- Continue eating as usual and track your daily calorie intake for about a week.
- Do not alter your eating habits to "eat healthier" during this period.
- **Using Stable Weight as a Benchmark**:
    
    - If your weight remains stable during the tracking period, your average daily calorie intake likely reflects your maintenance calorie needs.
    - If you are gaining weight, your calorie intake is above your maintenance needs; if losing weight, it is below.
- **Adjusting for Weight Goals**:
    
    - To achieve a healthy weight, adjust your calorie intake based on your tracking results.
    - Increase calories if underweight and decrease if overweight.
- **Consideration of Body Water Levels**:
    
    - Be aware of fluctuations in body weight due to water retention, especially in menstruating females.
    - Allow for a 5-pound buffer when assessing weight stability over several weeks.

#### Recommendations

##### How to Get to Healthy Weight if You are slightly Above Range?

- Simply cut your daily [[Calories]] by about 10%, around 2/3 of a pound a week
- As you get closer to desired weight, you will begin to slow down weight loss

##### How to Lose Large Amounts of Weight
- The 10% rule is nice but slow, and your body will slow down your metabolism, [[NEAT]] and raise hunger cravings.
- Thankfully, these effects ware off over time
- We can make use of [[maintenance phases]]: after every 10% loss of weight in an individual, up calories to maintenance levels for a few months to stave of the tendency to rebound back to previous weight.
- This also helps alleviate diet fatigue
- Make sure each weight loss phase is either to 10% loss or no longer than 3 months. Maintain for about the same amount of time as the loss period.
- No more than 0.5% - 1% of weight loss a week.

Some strategies to increase satiety while on weight loss period:
1. **More Protein**: High satiety, try to spread out consumption over the course of the day for best effects
2. **Focus on Veggies and Fruits**: High in fiber, low and calories, and easily add volume to meals
3. **Easy on Saturated Fats and Sugars**: People eat more tasty food than bland foods, these are very energy dense
4. **Don't Eat Out of Boredom**: self explanatory
